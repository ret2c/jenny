# Discover and rank targets

1. Inventory current workspace lanes, active ownership, local rejection reasons,
   submitted/accepted product history, unfinished goals, current ZDI policy,
   and direct operator exclusions.
2. Build a candidate pool biased toward current server, enterprise, security,
   ICS/SCADA, IoT, browser, OS, sandbox, VM, and mobile attack surfaces.
3. Use recent ZDI Published and Upcoming signals, current vendor policies,
   official releases, and primary architecture sources. Do not infer current
   buying interest solely from old purchases.
4. Apply hard gates from the scoring reference. Remove only proven hard
   exclusions; retain weak or crowded products as `DISCOURAGED` candidates.
5. Score surviving candidates. A new product ranks first only when it materially
   beats the best insufficiently reviewed existing lane.
6. Write `notes/target_scopes/YYYYMMDD/SCOPE_SHORTLIST.md` with five ranked
   candidates, factor scores, decisive evidence, likely economics, proof cost,
   collision pressure, and exact reason for rank.
7. Fully scope rank one using the named-product workflow. Do not fully scope the
   other four and do not acquire artifacts for any candidate.
8. Report the shortlist, full-scope path, goal mirror, validation result, and
   any unresolved buyer/acquisition caveat.

Stop with a `CANDIDATE` record rather than guessing when no product clears the
hard gates.
