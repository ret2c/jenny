SUBMITTED PATCH WATCH POLICY

Purpose

Preserve submission-versus-public-fix chronology for submitted ZDI cases
without adding a daemon, second scheduled loop, new review state, or mutable
submitted package.

Private watch record

The weekly manifest is filesystem-authoritative over every canonical historical
submission in `ZDI/_SUBMITTED`, `ZDI/_ACCEPTED`, and `ZDI/_REJECTED`. Accepted
and rejected cases remain submitted-case chronology and do not disappear from
public-fix monitoring. If the same numbered identity appears in more than one
terminal root, prefer ACCEPTED over REJECTED over SUBMITTED, retain every
observed location in the manifest, and scan the package only once. A matching
SQLite row enriches identity and chronology, but is optional; legacy packages
without rows remain normal in-scope inventory.

Moving the same numbered identity between those terminal roots changes its
buyer disposition and recorded location, not the research inventory identity.
The move is reconciled into a running manifest and never makes a completed
sweep due again. A new numbered identity, a changed title/product/version, or
an identity conflict still invalidates an early completed inventory snapshot.
Private records outside package directories contain:

- case or local package identity and exact submission timestamp;
- tested release, tag, source commit, and image/package identity;
- exact route, function, class, validator, sink, and distinctive error/message
  tokens worth checking;
- closest submitted or accepted siblings;
- last public check timestamp and result.

Check timing

The existing Midlane loop checks eligibility. One sweep is due Monday at
6:45 AM system-local time; the first later iteration catches up if Midlane was
unavailable. Research is resumable in bounded product batches. Do not create a
new timer, daemon, loop, browser session, or background worker.

An early run is current when its inventory-synchronizing finalization occurs on
Sunday or Monday, even if the bounded research started earlier. A far-early run
that also finalized before Sunday remains stale and cannot satisfy Monday.

Exact patch match

If public source now contains an exact or strongly matching fix, preserve:

- PR, issue, advisory, and commit URLs;
- PR creation, commit author, merge, release, and publication timestamps;
- the changed validation boundary and affected fields/routes;
- a direct comparison with the submitted root cause and impact; and
- whether the public work predates or postdates submission.

If the public work postdates submission, notify the operator promptly with the
chronology so the operator can update the ZDI case. If it predates submission,
route the case through the existing public-fix disposition rules. Do not infer
acceptance, rejection, duplication, or portal submission from a repository
change alone.

Durable no-repeat rule

`LIKELY_EXACT_FIX`, `FIX_RELEASED_AFTER_SUBMISSION`,
`PUBLIC_AFTER_SUBMISSION`, and `PUBLIC_BEFORE_SUBMISSION` are durable terminal
patch-watch results. Later
weekly manifests carry the prior result, sources, and chronology forward and
exclude that case from research batches. `POSSIBLE_FIX`, `NO_PUBLIC_CHANGE`,
source or record gaps, and new submissions are checked again.

Boundaries

- Submitted and frozen package bytes remain immutable.
- The three canonical terminal roots remain authoritative even when the review
  mailbox has no historical row.
- Watch notes stay private and outside external package folders.
- Public follow-up text is research evidence, not authority to reopen hunting.
- Do not hunt or package a publicly described unfixed follow-up as novel.
- Related findings are compared through PRE_FREEZE_PACKAGE_GATE.txt; one vendor
  patch touching several siblings does not automatically require one report.
